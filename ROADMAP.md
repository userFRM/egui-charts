# egui-charts Roadmap

[Feature status and progress](https://userfrm.github.io/egui-charts/docs/progress/) distinguishes independently verified, unverified, incomplete and missing behaviour. Checked items below describe implementation; the status page records independent verification.

The crate provides an engine without egui, an egui 0.37 chart widget, and terminal UI components. Checked items describe working capabilities, including public APIs that applications can compose. Unchecked items describe remaining library work. `egui_charts_terminal::Terminal`, in the unpublished `terminal` package, assembles the components on the host's feed and services and lets the application intercept their actions; it returns those it does not perform yet, and the demo still routes the components by hand.

## Chart Rendering Engine

### Chart Types

`ChartType` offers 21 chart types, with the rendering limits listed below.

- [x] Candles, OHLC bars, hollow candles and volume candles, with configurable rising and falling colours. Hollow and volume candles keep independent body, border and wick visibility and rising/falling colours, which persist in layouts and undo; series settings show and change the hollow and volume candle colours, and layouts saved before hollow or volume candles had these switches restore as they were drawn; volume candles and OHLC bars can also compare each close with the previous close. Added candle series accept a border width and per-bar body, wick and border colours; borders stay inside the body and default to disabled, on added volume candles too, while added hollow candles keep a 1.5 point border and take a positive width. The main series' last price line, its label and `series_last_value` of columns, hollow candles, and candles, volume candles and OHLC bars coloured by the previous close, take the direction of the last candle; added series compare the last bar's close with its open. Hollow and volume candles also take a bar's own body, border and wick colours. Candles, OHLC bars and volume candles that close at their open use rising colours, matching their last price line and label; volume bars, their tags and hover readout borders use the same direction rule.
- [x] Line, line with markers, step line, area, HLC area, baseline, columns and high-low charts; the Line, Step line, Line with markers, Area and Baseline charts each keep their own `PriceSource`, as the Columns chart does for the price each column reaches, and settings, templates and layouts saved with one shared source still draw it on all of them. The Line chart draws its line in one colour or in a gradient from a start colour at its leftmost segment in view to an end colour at its rightmost. Line-based main and added series run on to the plot edges while bars exist beyond them, with markers and volume on the bars in view. High-low charts draw every bar in one colour and print each bar's high and low once zoomed in far enough to fit them, with independent label visibility, colour and size that persist in layouts and undo, and labels in the price scale's text colour until a colour is chosen and at 1 to 256 points; layouts and chart settings templates saved before these controls restore without labels. Added high-low series tag their last value and draw their price line in their bar colour. Added column series apply their configured hit tolerance around their painted bounds, including flat bodies, dense bars and single-point histories; zero uses the body bounds and all hits stay inside the plot. Columns draw in rising and falling colours of their own at 50% opacity, kept apart from the candle colours and following the theme until edited, with their last value tag and price line at full opacity; layouts and templates saved before them draw in their candle body colours. Baseline fills fade from 28% opacity at the pane edge to 5% at the base by default and accept independent colours at the pane edge and the base on each price side, in the Symbol settings too, including point overrides, while older settings keep flat fills. Area and baseline plots support point markers with independent radii for main and added series. Added series retain their own curved and patterned lines, OHLC open-tick visibility and thin bars, area gradient endpoints, relative gradients and fill inversion. Line-based added series pulse continuously or on last-bar updates on their own pane and scale; expired update pulses stop requesting repaints.
- [x] Main-series conflation has a configurable physical-pixel threshold or can be disabled. `Chart::prepare_conflation` prepares the shared render cache before a dense view; groups retain price extremes, gaps and per-bar colours and refresh after data, price-source or grouping changes.
- [x] Heikin-Ashi, Renko, Kagi, line break, point and figure, and range-bar transformations and rendering. Heikin-Ashi retains independent body, border and wick visibility and rising/falling colours; Symbol settings also select original-only scale prices or the default pair of original and transformed closes, and series settings show and change the same colours. These choices persist in layouts and undo; layouts saved before them restore with the original close alone. The time-independent types hide volume, with the volume study's price-axis tag, tooltip and data window rows, the volume in the data window and the accessible description, and the columns and value range of a volume study pinned to a scale, and their automatic scale keeps the last price line and label in view while the newest element is shown. Renko, Kagi, line break and point and figure label the last real price on the scale by default, or the close of their newest element when chosen in the Symbol settings.
- [x] Renko box sizing and wicks, line-break lookback, and price-based chart parameters are configurable. Renko bricks and range bars complete on the close or trade that reaches their size for decimal sizes such as 0.1, 0.05 and 0.01, and their prices are counted in sizes instead of added up. ATR and percentage sizes resolve from the loaded history once it gives them a positive value, and keep it while bars stream in, when chart settings, undo and redo leave their method unchanged and while the replay cursor moves forward; a replay resolves them from the bars before its cursor when it starts and after a step back, and gives back the sizes it found when it ends. Until enough history is loaded the chart names the bars it waits for, and other settings still apply.
- [x] Market Profile (TPO) uses exchange sessions or calendar days, with letters in their blocks by default or plain blocks, initial balance, point of control, value area and composite profiles. A profile can split at a selected period, keeping its session's period letters in both parts, each with its own levels and statistics; layouts and chart settings retain these splits. Merging neighbouring parts removes their split, and the profile menu resets every merge and split as one undoable step; autoscale fits the full displayed distributions and cropped profiles retain their session and central-level readouts. POC, value-area and optional midpoint captions keep a gutter at the first or offscreen session opening, separate nearby levels and reveal their prices on hover. Profile period also combines sessions by ISO week or calendar month, retaining the first session's open and initial balance and calculating levels and statistics over all sessions. Explicit period splits divide these profiles and menu merges join them; one-day profiles choose their hours (chart, regular, extended, pre-market, post-market, each segment or custom sessions) and week and month profiles keep the chart's. Split display keeps each period in its own column in time order, with the same levels and statistics, and profile-menu clicks follow the displayed cells. Symbol settings choose four period colours, the first two shading the periods lettered A to Z and the last two those lettered a to z, or one solid block colour, outside-value-area opacity and initial balance visibility; the balance extensions remain independent of its marker. Point of control, value area high and low and the open price mark each have a visibility switch, and an optional closing price mark follows the last bar of each profile. Poor highs and poor lows switch separately and can extend from completed profiles, keeping the centre of their extreme row after the profile scrolls offscreen, until a later bar trades beyond the extreme price; the older combined switch restores with both marks. An optional volume profile beside each TPO profile shares supplied bar volume across its touched rows, follows the theme and outside-value-area opacity, and stays off for older saves. The Time Price Opportunity study offers the block colours, outside-area opacity, level switches, balance period and extensions, poor-extreme controls, volume profile and split display as inputs. Merged profiles also split at the selected period in one undoable edit, keeping the periods before and after it in separate profiles. On time-based charts without a chart-type profile menu, the study profiles have the same merge, period split and reset menu, with edits saved as study inputs and one history step per command. Older saved settings keep the previous palette, 45 percent opacity, session periods, packed rows and visible balance. Merges and splits are dropped when the period length, the row size or the profile period changes in the settings, and one undo restores them. Automatic rows take their size from the price range of the latest 300 bars up to the last visible bar, about 80 rows of whole instrument ticks, so the chart type and the study share them and zooming or scrolling the price scale keeps every profile's levels.
- [x] Volume Footprint and Session Volume Profile aggregate supplied executions on time, tick, volume, range and delta intervals with an instrument tick size; bars without complete retained executions use candles. Instrument volume decimals describe unabbreviated numbers; compact footprint cells and statistics keep one decimal and ladders up to two when those decimals are fewer. Legends, readouts, volume tags, crosshair labels over the volume area, profile rows, symbol details, time and sales, Zig Zag volume labels, the accessible chart summary and detector texts keep at least two decimals in compact units. Session profiles require `studies`, divide the chart by the chart's hours, the instrument's regular or extended hours, its pre-market or post-market hours alone or each overnight, pre-market, regular and post-market segment apart, or up to five daily sessions of their own in any zone, whose shared hours form a profile of their own or stay in both and which can be clipped to the instrument's regular hours, and group executions into about 24 rows by default, a chosen row count or whole ticks per row; autoscale includes their full price rows after range edits, and manual crops retain session and POC/value-area readouts at the drawn centre and edge prices. Completed sessions' POC and value-area extensions end at the first later traded range that reaches their drawn price. Hosts can restrict the chart type menu and favourite strip to declared historical or live executions for the resolved instrument; unavailable favourites stay saved.
- [x] Leave bars outside the chosen session hours out of session volume profiles instead of joining them to the session before.
- [x] Kagi lines change thickness at the shoulder or waist they cross within a continuing leg. The first line starts at the first close and takes the direction of the first move beyond the reversal amount, up or down. Thick and thin lines have their own colours, apart from the candle colours, which follow the theme while unedited.
- [x] Point and figure charts take a high/low or close-only source from chart parameters and settings, draw their columns in colours of their own, and can build one box charts one step back, continuing a column of one symbol with the reversal.
- [x] Renko bricks can hide their borders and take rising and falling border and wick colours of their own, and line break lines can hide their borders and take rising and falling border colours; unset borders use the brick's or line's colour at full opacity, so enabling a custom border starts palette picks at full opacity too. Unset wicks use the brick's colour, and saved custom border colours keep their opacity.
- [x] Range charts build their bars from the supplied executions, after the OHLC approximation of the bars before the one holding the first held trade, and fall back to the approximation alone while the chart holds none; replay follows the same rule at its cursor, and `Chart::range_bar_source` reports which one is shown. The approximation takes each bar from its open to the extreme nearer the open, then to the other extreme and to the close, and a range bar closes at its high when a rise completes it and at its low when a fall does. Each range bar is drawn over its whole range, from its low to its high, filled or as a thin bar with open and close ticks, and the bars that a move across a price gap closed without a trade can be outlined as phantom bars. The bar in progress is drawn in rising and falling colours of its own until its range is complete.
- [x] Volume candle bodies scale with their share of the largest visible volume, narrowing to the wick width at zero volume.
- [x] HLC area charts draw high, low and close lines with their own colour, width and style, and two fills with their own colour and opacity, which a palette colour keeps; high line, low line and each fill can be hidden. The close line follows the bar closes whatever price source the line and area types plot. The settings persist in layouts and undo, and layouts and chart templates saved with the former fill opacity and close line width restore as they were drawn. Kagi stroke widths are exposed in the settings.
- [x] TPO profiles can show a statistics table below them with the point of control, value area, initial balance range, TPO counts below and above the point of control, rotation factor and range extension and, on request, the high-low range, value area range, initial balance high and low, total TPO count and total volume; the table and each row have a switch on the chart type and the study.
- [x] TPO single-print highlights and the midpoint caption and marker are off by default and switch on from the chart's settings or the study's inputs. Charts and studies saved before the midpoint had a setting restore with it shown.
- [x] The TPO study keeps its profiles to the trading day, the regular hours, the pre-market or post-market alone, or custom daily hours in any zone, counting only the bars inside them and keeping custom hours' periods whole on the period grid, and its extended-hours split profiles each segment of the day from the bars of its hours.
- [x] TPO profiles can caption the initial balance high and low, the opening and closing prices, poor highs and lows and single-print runs besides the point of control, value area and midpoint, each with its own switch, write each caption's price at the instrument's precision and tag the newest profile's captioned levels on the price scale. The Close caption follows the closing mark at its exact price in its direction colour and starts off. Charts saved before these settings keep their captions.

### Pan, Zoom and Cursor

- [x] Cursor-centred wheel zoom, horizontal pan, drag pan, touch pan and pinch zoom. A pinch zooms around the midpoint between the fingers and follows its movement, so the bars under both fingers stay under them. Plain dragging remains available after a drawing selection is released or its pane is removed. Rolling the wheel away zooms in on time and price axes; rolling it toward zooms out, including Ctrl/Command wheel and trackpad scrolling, while axis drags, Shift+wheel pan and pinches retain their directions. Over the price axis of the main series, of a study pane or of an added series the wheel zooms that scale alone, around the pointer. Scroll and scale gesture groups can be disabled independently without changing their individual permissions; disabling scrolling stops its momentum, and the scale switch also covers axis drags and resets in every pane.
- [x] Touch swipes released while moving coast by default with configurable damping. Mouse drags stop on release unless their separate coast option is enabled. The demo's Scrolling menu switches touch and mouse coasting. A drag that came to rest before its release stays where it was dropped. A press, a touch, the wheel and the pan and zoom keys stop the scrolling. With the default options a flick continues at the speed of the drag, up to 3000 points per second, and settles in under two seconds. The distance it travels does not depend on the frame rate or on pauses in painting.
- [x] Axis drag scaling, double-click reset, box zoom and chart-owned navigation buttons, which show while the pointer is over the main pane unless the configuration or the Canvas settings choose always or never. Box zoom ends after one successful selection, keeps its mode for a selection too small to zoom, and yields to a drawing tool or the eraser; the demo's Zoom in button follows the chart's mode, and picking a cursor ends it. A selection can start in any pane's plot and zooms that pane's values with the time range, and zoom back gives each scale it set its fit or manual range back, a locked price-to-bar ratio included; it keeps the held value readout off the bars it selects, and the zoom-in cursor shows over the plots only. Every price axis zooms around the price where its drag began and returns to automatic scaling on a double-click. A time-axis drag zooms around the bar that was pressed by its whole movement, whatever frames it spans, and coming back restores the view. Fit content includes every loaded bar series across panes, including hidden series and whitespace, reserves the configured bar or pixel right margin, and respects spacing limits. An empty main series uses the union of added-series timestamps for its horizontal mapping. Live updates preserve the view on valued bars; filling a whitespace tail in its own slot follows only when both shift options are enabled and the view is near the live edge. Removing the last bars, a whitespace tail included, through a pop, data replacement or replay seek keeps any pending start index, and the right offset of a view near the live edge. A scrolled-back view keeps the bars it shows in place when bars after its right edge are removed or inserted, by a pop, an update, data replacement, a replay cutoff moved back or a feed pause and resume; once the bar at its right edge goes, the newest bar before it takes its place. Appended bars retain their new-bar follow policy. Bars arriving between frames, older or newer and in any number, on a chart type that rebuilds its bars or on a chart without main bars, keep a following view at its live edge, including with a fixed right edge, and a scrolled-back view on its bars. Consecutive view movements by key or navigation button are one undo step, which restores the view from before them, and leave redo available. Undoing a user action that changes the interval restores its prior spacing and scroll position when the view moved after the action, including after the restored interval's history loads and its price axis is sized; redo restores the view left by undo. A later request to move the view replaces a waiting restoration, whether the restored interval's bars arrive before or after it; tracking stays at the live edge, and a call that changes nothing keeps the restoration. Axes with drag scaling enabled show a horizontal resize cursor on time and a vertical one on price; disabling the scale group or the axis drag restores the plot cursor for the crosshair style. Selecting a drawing tool keeps axis drags and their resize cursors available without placing a drawing; a placement or edit already in progress retains its drag when it crosses an axis. Drag-to-draw and freehand tools show their crosshair over the plot and while drawing. A range set before the first frame is fitted at the plot width even when bars are merged before that frame: time and synced ranges keep their dates, and logical ranges keep their bars at their new indices after older bars arrive or a bar limit evicts some. A later host or user view change still replaces the range. Appended bars retain their new-bar follow policy after the first frame.
- [x] Programmatic crosshair positions target a bar series in its own price units, scale and pane, with labels in their target axis columns on either side, pointer precedence and matching image exports. A hidden target keeps the time guide in every pane and restores its saved price readout when shown. Invalid positions and removed targets clear the crosshair. Positions set from code persist inside layouts with crosshair synchronization on or off, and return after a linked pointer leaves.
- [x] Cross, dot and arrow cursor modes, the dot standing in for the hidden pointer over the plots, styled crosshair lines, price/time labels and OHLC snapping. One-point dots in axis-aligned lines and dotted construction guides cover at least one device pixel in each direction at every scale, faded by their device width when thinner than one device pixel. Dotted lines and construction guides centred on a pane edge keep a lit pixel inside its clip at every display scale. Both lines and both axis labels have independent visibility switches; saved options without label switches inherit each label's line switch; hidden mode suppresses every crosshair element. Independent time-scale filters exclude whitespace and hidden-only values from crosshair indices without removing stored data; visible series and study values keep their slots eligible. Line-based main and added series accept independent crosshair marker radii, fills, border colours and border widths, including on independent scales and in separate panes. Markers start hidden; enabling them without colour overrides keeps the point's line colour and a ring in the background at the marker or the secondary pane's fill. Added-series crosshair markers avoid allocating chart parameters on hovered frames.
- [x] Main-pane crosshair snapping includes visible studies and added series on their own scales for time-based charts. Close-only snapping compares visible main and added-series closes on every chart type, preferring the main series and then added-series draw order on equal screen distances. While a drawing tool is active, the main-pane crosshair, its price label and the reported pointer price show where a click places the next anchor, following the drawing magnet's strength, distance, Ctrl or Command inversion and study snapping rather than the crosshair mode. Dragging a drawing's anchor shows the price it takes by the same rules, and size and rotation handles keep the crosshair on the pointer. Study panes follow the same rules with their own values. The demo's cursor menu switches close-only snapping on and the chart saves it.
- [x] Hover readouts and the data window show bar and study values. The status line, pane legends and data window read the bar under the chart's crosshair, whether it follows the pointer, a linked chart or a position set from code, and the latest bar without one. On Heikin Ashi charts they, the tooltip and the accessible description describe the drawn candle, with the change between averaged closes, while studies read the source bars. By default a touch held for a configurable delay shows the readout, which follows the finger without panning and stays after release until the next tap, or ends on release when the host chooses; mouse hover is unchanged, and live-edge following is separate. An activated readout owns its contact through release, so it neither clicks series nor counts as manual navigation. Holds work without keyboard focus, window focus loss cancels them, and resizing clears a latched position outside the plot. A latched price button keeps its value through a tap and returns the price action to the host. Crosshair notifications report the held or latched readout, including its activation delay and exit.
- [x] Mouse drags can start kinetic scrolling through their own option, independently of touch drags.
- [x] The box-zoom overlay uses the theme's information colour with a translucent fill.
- [x] The floating, held and tracking readouts lead with the instrument's symbol. Floating and held readouts and the data window name the horizontal value from its scale: Time for time scales, Maturity for maturities and a host name or no name for other numeric domains.
- [x] `Chart::data_table` and `DataTableDialog` show the loaded bars and visible study plots in a table, opened from the chart menu.

### Scales and Panes

- [x] Linear, logarithmic, percentage, indexed-to-100 and inverted price scales, autoscale, manual ranges and scale options. Secondary-pane crosshair labels use their axis units on relative scales, including shared studies and explicit percentage formatters. Series autoscale providers and attached primitives can reserve independent top and bottom logical-pixel margins, combined by edge maximum within each pane and scale and added to fractional margins after resizing; manual ranges ignore them, studies without values in view add no fallback prices while attached primitives still contribute, a scale with no price to fit keeps its range without reapplying pixel margins, and a pane too short for a request keeps its ordinary fit, including the room for markers. Fractional margins that together leave no room for prices are ignored by the fit and are kept as set when the chart saves, restores and applies its own settings. Main and added series independently style their 0% or indexed-100 reference lines with colour, width, pattern and visibility; saved styles retain these settings. Marker autoscaling reserves room for shapes, outlines and labels of visible markers positioned from bars or prices at their configured size on main and secondary panes; hidden markers and disabled autoscaling add no room.
- [x] Time-axis tick hierarchy, price marks that default to the instrument tick grid with host overrides, exchange time zones, session breaks through every pane of an intraday chart in a chosen colour, width and pattern, and extended-hours shading with pre-market and post-market tints of their own. Symbol settings switch session highlights, choose phase colours and opacity, and retain them in templates and Apply to all; new charts use orange before regular hours, blue afterward and purple overnight, each phase with a switch of its own, while saved backgrounds retain their colours. Sessions distinguish overnight windows from pre-market and post-market windows, with their own market phase and tint. Chart requests, replay, alerts, undo and saved layouts retain regular, extended or all hours; the time axis offers overnight hours where supported. Regular-hours clocks keep schedules without regular windows closed, while date overrides can open them.
- [x] Linear numeric horizontal scales for strikes and maturities use actual sample keys without placeholder bars; series, panes, ticks and cursor conversions share their positions. Time charts retain ordinal spacing. Custom horizontal domains accept categorical and structured host keys ordered by their type, with shared formatting, typed lookup and annotation placement; unknown keys are rejected and repeated keys update one slot. Drawing anchors and their saved coordinates follow keys on custom and numeric axes, including whitespace, regardless of the chart timeframe. Numeric and custom-key domains retain their initial spacing unit across updates, preserving pixel distances per key and the keys in a scrolled-back view, including historical insertions and added-series domains. Numeric bar bodies are capped by the closest neighbouring keys in view at the configured candle-width ratio, for main and added series. Volume and automatic study bars also fit these gaps, keeping their own width ratios.
- [x] Chart-wide time, price, percentage and volume formatters, plus per-series price formatters. Non-finite volumes and order quantities show a dash. Legend, study and symbol-header volumes carry rounded values into the next K, M or B unit, written with that unit's decimals; quote-button quantities carry the same way, trim decimal zeros and retain exact fractions below 1,000. Saved series styles declare price, abbreviated volume, raw percentage or fractional display with precision and minimum movement, independently of scale transforms. Their prices retain the chart's thousands grouping and their changes retain built-in signs; host formatter text stays as supplied. Logarithmic volume ticks retain their lower decades without changing linear abbreviated-unit spacing. Explicit series and chart price formatters take precedence; a shared axis takes tick settings from its first visible series, and each series retains its own tags and readouts. Price and percentage tick hooks receive the complete ordered list for each axis in its displayed units, including edge ticks; main and independent series axes measure their width from that same list, while crosshair and price tags keep individual-value formatting. Time tick hooks receive the current locale and display-time context and can defer individual ticks to the normal locale-aware text without changing crosshair formatting. Drawing prices and price changes use their pane's raw-price formatter, including measurements, forecasts, position levels and rectangle heights. Position axis tags in separate series panes follow the owning series' formatter and display grid. Built-in drawing prices and price changes group thousands with the chart's separator in panes with a price series, including Fibonacci prices drawn by a study; study-only panes retain their own units. Fractional prices use halves through 256ths or thirty-seconds with half, quarter or eighth steps, rounding once to that step. The chart settings choose fractional precision for the axis, crosshair, tags and readouts; the choice persists through undo, layouts and instrument changes, and explicit series formats and host formatters keep precedence. An instrument can be quoted in a fraction, also from an HTTP symbol, which labels its price scale while no precision is chosen.
- [x] Locale-aware date names and configurable date patterns. Forecast dates use the chart's display time zone and crosshair time formatter, including host formatting and clock visibility. Language codes alone use their most common region's names, and language and region match in any letter case.
- [x] Chart-owned panes share the time axis and support resizing, reordering, stretch sizing, collapse and maximise controls. The price pane's own menu moves it up or down and collapses it, as study panes' menus do. While a pane is maximized, its menu disables moves and collapse, and its header cannot collapse it, so restoring panes keeps their earlier order and heights without extra undo steps. Ctrl/Command plus double-click collapses or restores the pointed pane, including its header, and preserves sizing, focus, undo and saved layouts; outside every pane it stays an ordinary double-click. Dragging, scrolling and pinching over the plot of any pane move the shared time scale as over the price pane. With the price pane collapsed, the other panes fill the chart and keep that navigation. Removing any pane, including the original price pane, deletes its members and annotations while preserving surviving panes and their horizontal mapping; the final pane is kept. Pane menus disable removal and merging when a contained study disallows user editing, and their member controls keep read-only studies in place, including on the price pane. Host pane operations remain available. Snapshots preserve layouts without the original pane; the main series stays out of such a layout while its bars keep arriving from the host or the feed for the studies and the time axis.
- [x] Main and added series and studies can move between panes. The main series retains its data, horizontal mapping, price scale, annotations and pointer readouts in its destination; snapshots and undo preserve placement. Studies and added series can use separate panes and independent scales. Named left, right, additional and hidden overlay assignments share options and ranges only within their pane; painting, axis controls, hit tests and coordinate queries follow those assignments after pane moves, and snapshots retain each pane's named scales. The demo menu's Samples entries open synthetic strike and maturity charts with named horizontal readouts and no volume row, and preserve the price workspace on return.

Unused axes hide by default and return when a source becomes visible; the policy and layout survive saving and undo. Axis and plot menus expose these controls in the library and demo, and chart settings edit the main series' pane. One price-scale menu serves every price axis: a study pane's axis opens it with the rows of its own scale, and a touch held on the main price axis, a study pane's axis, an added series' axis or the time axis opens that axis' menu as a secondary click does.
- [x] Scale settings control currency and unit labels, shown by default only while the pointer is over the price scale or their menu is open, with an always-visible choice; saved layouts and settings keep their previous visibility. They also control scale-mode and price-action buttons, price-to-bar ratio, label overlap, high and low tags and lines independently through settings and the price axis menu, price lines that remain visible without a price axis, the last price line's width and pattern, the previous close line's colour, pattern and width, bid and ask tags and lines apart, a last price tag that adds the change from the previous session's close, and symbol, study and extended-session value colours. The chart configuration styles the high and low, bid and ask and previous close lines and draws an optional line and tag at the average close of the bars in view. With no colour set, the previous close line and its tag use mid grey (#555555) in light themes and secondary text in dark themes; the settings colour well shows the same colour on opening and after Reset, while explicit colours stay through theme changes. The price scale header lists host-supplied unit conversions under its currencies, including when it shows only a unit, and emits separate currency and unit intents for the host to supply converted bars and metadata. The demo menu converts synthetic USD prices to US cents through the host feed contract, including history, executions and book prices, and keeps the unit through replay and saved layouts.
- [x] A double-click on the plot of a study pane or of the pane holding the main series maximises or restores it, while a double-click that places, finishes or selects a drawing or lands on a series, an axis or the status line keeps its own effect.

- [x] Added-series value tags and last-value queries use configured scale precision when no formatter is supplied, otherwise the nearest tick label's decimals with at least two; saved numeric display declarations also control their tags and readouts.
- [ ] Draw symbol-name and study-name tags on the price scale while their value tags are hidden; the names otherwise appear only inside shown value tags.
- [x] Scale font size, axis line colour and pane-button visibility settings apply to the price, study and time axes and the pane controls. The scale font size and family reach every label and tag on those axes, the crosshair's included, and no other chart text.

### Replay

- [x] Bar replay hides future main-series bars, supports start-bar/date/random selection, stepping, autoplay, speed and seek, and restores live data on exit, keeping the bars and depth history on screen while the feed catches up. Leaving a bar or market replay shows the newest bar of every replayed chart at the configured right offset and keeps the zoom, however far the view was scrolled back during the session. Multi-day session bars complete at their last trading window close, which also ends their bar-close countdown. On time-based intervals, the demo supplies its newest bar, quote and book before execution backfill completes. Trade-based intervals still rebuild their bars on resume. Changing the demo chart interval keeps a finer compatible replay step and rebuilds its source, or steps by the new chart interval; the update list always includes the active step. ## Provided by the host

The crate renders supplied data and exposes interfaces for application services. The application supplies market data, broker execution, accounts and durable storage. These interfaces also allow services to run remotely.

- `Datafeed`, `SymbolSearch`, `Quotes` and `Marks` connect instrument discovery, history, live prices, executions, depth and chart events. Demo marks follow newly streamed and older paged synthetic bars with stable symbol/time identities. Feed queries also supply metrics, options volume, listed option expiries and live or historical option chains with quotes, open interest, implied volatility and Greeks, analyst targets, symbol details, news, calendars, screeners and hotlists. Analyst target projections are dotted from their publication close, coloured against that close, and filled on each side with saved positive and negative colours whose opacity affects only the fill; the study catalogue offers them as the Analyst price forecast study, kept in step with the chart setting, whose legend row hides, removes and edits them. The host supplies market heatmap universes and metrics, and the terminal updates their prices and changes from `Quotes`. The headless `OptionChain` handle retains the latest validated snapshot and error; the host supplies all option data. `OptionExposure::gamma` computes exposure per 1% underlying move by expiry and strike, counting contracts with missing inputs and using positive call and negative put signs as a convention. `Datafeed::option_exposure` supplies gamma, delta, vanna or charm snapshots with their units and model label; `OptionExposureHandle` retains the latest valid observation and prefers it unchanged over computed gamma.
- `Broker` supplies real or paper-account state, trading rules, execution and account updates. Trading components submit actions and render the reported orders, positions, fills and history.
- `Storage` supplies layouts, drawings, settings and template documents; an application may use local or remote storage and manage accounts, synchronisation and sharing. With a host that keeps revisions, every document but the recent symbols list is written over the version read, and a refused write keeps the unsaved document until it is settled with a `ConflictChoice`.
- `Alerts` stores and evaluates alert conditions and reports changes and triggers. The crate edits models, including the notification schedules a service applies, and draws levels, dialogs, logs and toasts; the application supplies sound and external delivery.
- `Signals` supplies markers, levels and zones with category visibility and click callbacks. Captions yield to price bars, signal borders and other captions, prioritising recent markers; hover retains the labels and details of coincident events. Applications can deliver iceberg, absorption, spoofing, large-order and other interpreted order-flow events through it.
- `Notifications` and `Chats` supply notification records and conversation messages; `Chats` can also answer a room's older messages a page at a time, and the chats panel writes message times in the host's zone under a separator for each day and draws text between code markers as a monospace block that keeps its lines and indentation. Where the host allows, a message's author opens a menu to reply to the message, copy the host's link to it or ignore the author, whose messages stay hidden until the user stops ignoring them, or view the author's profile, whose card can start or open a direct chat with them. Notifications can open a profile, room, post or news story by its host identifier, and `ChatsPanel::open_room` shows a room by it, listing one the service has not pushed. The ideas composer requires a title and description for an idea and only the text for a short post, and carries direction, tags and public or private visibility, which feed entries show, with videos on a tab of their own; entries show the host's like and comment counts and ask the host to like, comment on and follow them, and the feed pages Liked, Commented and Followed views; the toolbar exposes independent idea publishing and snapshot posting intents. The demo publishes session-local ideas and chart images, opens their notifications in the matching feed, shows an entry in full from its Open link or notification, and logs each sent chat message with a notification that opens its room. Publishing and sharing actions are application intents; `Posts` resolves the links of post and idea drawings through `Chart::set_post_source`, and the chart never fetches a link itself.
- `StrategyReport` supplies the trade list, equity and performance inputs for the report panel; `CompanyFinancials` supplies financial tables. Applications also provide translations, additional fonts, image loaders and platform save/clipboard facilities.
